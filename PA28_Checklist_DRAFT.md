# Emergencies

## Engine Failure After Takeoff
*lf the engine fails after take-off you should (as soon as you notice the loss of power):*
1.  Lower the Nose and Maintain the Aircraft’s Gliding Speed
2.  Close the Throttle
3.  Select the Most Suitable Landing Area, Compatible With Obstructions and Wind
    *   Direction; Preferably Straight Ahead or Within 30° Either Side
4.  Maintain Speed and Use Flap as Necessary
5.  lf Time Permits: - Mixture to I.C.O., Fuel Off, Fuel Pump Off, Magnetos Off, Door
    *   Unlocked and Harnesses Tight, Mayday Call, Master Switch Off
6.  After Touchdown Leave the Aircraft as Quickly as Possible

---

## Crash Drill
1.  Mixture I.C.O, Fuel Pump Off
2.  Fuel Selector Off
3.  Magnetos Off
4.  Brakes Off
5.  Harnesses Very Tight
6.  Hatches Unlatched
7.  Master Switch Off (After Electrical Services are No Longer Required)

---

## Engine Fire During Start
1.  Starter - Crank Engine
2.  Mixture - Idle Cut Off
3.  Throttle - Open
4.  Electric Fuel Pump - Off
5.  Fuel Selector - Off
6.  Abandon Aircraft if Fire Continues

---

## Engine Fire In The Air
1.  Fuel Selector - Off
2.  Throttle - Closed
3.  Mixture - Idle Cut-Off
4.  Electric Fuel Pump - Check Off
5.  Heater and Defroster - Off
6.  Proceed with Power Off Landing Procedure

---

## Electrical Fire In The Air
1.  Master Switch Off, Alternator Off
2.  Vents - Open
3.  Cabin Heat - Off
4.  Land as soon as Practicable.

---

## Cabin Fire In The Air
1.  Master Switch Off
2.  Vents, Cabin Air/Heat Closed
3.  Use Fire Extinguisher and Then Ventilate The Cabin
4.  Land as Soon as Possible.

---

## Forced Landing Without Power
1.  Adopt Glide and Trim for Correct Gliding Speed
2.  Check Wind Direction
3.  Select Landing Area and 1000' Area
4.  Plan Circuit
5.  Complete Failure Checks:
    *   Switch Tanks
    *   Electric Fuel Pump On
    *   Mixture Rich
    *   Carb. Heat Change Position
    *   Magnetos on Both
    *   Primer Locked
6.  Mayday Call on Radio
7.  Complete Crash Drill
8.  Use Full Flap for the Touchdown if Possible
9.  Apply Heavy Braking

---

## Forced Landing With Power
1.  Assess the Time Available and Check Fuel State, Hours of Daylight and Rate of
    *   Weather Deterioration
2.  Check Wind Direction
3.  Select Suitable Landing Area
4.  Carry Out an Inspection Run at Slow Safe Cruising Speed; Initially at 500' to Check
    *   for Obstructions and Secondly at 100' Over the Landing Area to Reassess the
    *   Approach Path, Landing Surface and Overshoot Area (if time permits)
5.  Set up For a Short or Soft Field Landing (as appropriate)

---

## Ditching
1.  Set up Minimum Rate of Descent (75 Kts)
2.  Mayday Call
3.  Secure or Jettison Heavy Objects
4.  With High Winds and Heavy Seas: Approach Into Wind.
    *   With Light Winds and Heavy Swells: Approach Parallel to Swells.
5.  Unlatch the Door
6.  Touchdown in a Level Attitude
7.  Evacuate Through the Cabin Door; if Necessary Open Window to Equalise Pressure

**Notes:**
1.  Ideally, land ahead and either side of a small ship.
2.  Do not inflate life jackets in the cockpit.

---

## Low-Voltage/Electrical Failure
1.  Check Ammeter, if Reading Zero, Alternator Off
2.  Reset Circuit Breaker
3.  Alternator On
4.  Reduce Electrical Load

**Notes:**
1.  lf total electrical failure occurs, keep an extra good lookout. Land when safe to do so,
    *   clear the runway as quickly as possible and phone ATC at once.

---

## Radio Failure
1.  Check Correct Frequency
2.  Check Squelch Setting (if applicable)
3.  Check Volume
4.  Check Microphone Plug Secure
5.  Check Circuit Breaker
6.  Try to Communicate Using the Other PTT Switch

**Notes:**
1.  lf the radio is unserviceable: flash the landing light on approach and land on visual
    *   signals.
2.  lf flying outside of the circuit, follow lost comm procedure from AIP.

## Uncertain of Position / Lost Procedure

### Uncertain of Position
Inform ATC immediately on the frequency you are in communication with.
Check flight plan, log, compass and heading for gross errors; calculate DR position; from
flight time decide radius of circle of uncertainty; mark on map using DR position as Centre;
estimate new heading from DR position to regain destination or track. Utilise Nav aids
including moving map if available.

### Lost Procedure
a) Radio Available
Check frequency selection; declare that you are lost; make maximum use of aids
available; consider increasing height to improve range (taking account of controlled
airspace). R/T procedure: urgency or distress call to obtain fix. You are no longer lost.
Follow the ATC instructions in full.
b) Radio Not Available
i. Check remaining fuel and calculate endurance; consider daylight remaining, weather
tendency, nature of terrain in relation to probable position, minimum safe altitude; recheck flight log for errors; switch transponder, if available, to emergency code.
ii. Map reading from ground to map and try to identify the likely area from the nature of
the terrain before checking other features. Look for prominent features in the distance
as well as nearby; adhere to a calculated heading and keep map properly orientated.
iii. Decide on a positive course of action; relative merits of square search, flight towards
prominent or line feature outside of circle of uncertainty; consider precautionary
landing (see Ex 17) if you are short of fuel or are unable to continue the flight.

*End of Emergency Checklist*
